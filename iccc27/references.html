Conference overview

The International Conference on Computational Creativity (ICCC) is the leading international forum for research on how computers can participate in creative activities. It brings together computer scientists, artists, designers, cognitive scientists, philosophers, and other researchers to study how creative processes work and to develop computational systems that can generate, evaluate, or support ideas, artworks, music, stories, designs, and other forms of creative expression. The conference considers both the possibilities and the limitations of machine creativity, with a strong emphasis on collaboration between people and intelligent systems. Growing out of international workshops in the late 1990s, ICCC has been held annually since 2010 in locations across Europe, North America, South America, Asia, and Australia.

Technical overview

ICCC is a multidisciplinary research venue focused on the computational modelling, implementation, and evaluation of creative behaviour. Its work spans generative systems, machine learning, evolutionary computation, search and optimisation, knowledge representation, natural language generation, computer vision, robotics, human–computer interaction, and computational models of cognition. Research commonly addresses how systems represent concepts and goals, explore spaces of possible artefacts, produce novelty and value, evaluate or explain their outputs, and interact with human creators. The field also examines evaluation methodologies, system autonomy, co-creativity, interpretability, datasets, and the limits of applying computational criteria to creative work.

Arts-oriented overview

For artists and creative practitioners, ICCC is a place to explore what happens when computational systems become creative collaborators, instruments, materials, or audiences. The conference considers how artists work with algorithms, generative models, robots, interactive systems, and data, while asking how authorship, intention, agency, originality, taste, and cultural meaning change in these contexts. It welcomes practice-based and critical perspectives alongside technical research, treating creative technologies not only as tools for producing new artefacts but also as ways to question how creativity is understood, who or what can be considered an author, and how people and machines might make work together.

Historical references

  1. Cardoso, A., Veale, T., & Wiggins, G. A. (2009). “Converging on the Divergent: The History and Future of the International Joint Workshops in Computational Creativity.” AI Magazine, 30(3), 15–22. The key historical overview, tracing computational creativity from the 1990s workshops through the development of the ICCC series. DOI · ResearchGate
  2. Proceedings of ICCC 2010, Lisbon, Portugal. The inaugural ICCC proceedings. The preface explains that the conference marked the tenth anniversary of the computational-creativity event series and describes the preceding IJWCC workshops. Proceedings PDF
  3. Association for Computational Creativity — ICCC conference history. The official ACC overview states that the ICCC series has been organized by ACC since 2010 and lists the past host locations. Official ICCC history
  4. Association for Computational Creativity — ICCC Proceedings archive. The official archival source for the conference’s peer-reviewed research record, with editions published from 2010 onward. Official proceedings archive
  5. DBLP — International Conference on Computational Creativity. An independent bibliographic record containing proceedings, editors, dates, locations, ISBNs, and persistent records for conference editions. DBLP conference index

References on the conference’s development

  1. Proceedings of ICCC 2012, Dublin, Ireland. The preface describes ICCC 2012 as part of a conference series that grew out of a decade of workshops, following the first conference in Portugal in 2010 and the second in Mexico in 2011. Proceedings PDF
  2. Cunha, J. M., et al. (2020). “Understanding and Strengthening the Computational Creativity Community.” Published in the ICCC 2020 proceedings. It analyzes ICCC’s community, authorship, reviewing practices, diversity, and development from 2010–2020. Paper PDF
  3. Official ICCC 2026 conference page, Coimbra. Documents the most recent Coimbra edition, including its scope, program structure, proceedings, workshops, early-career activities, and attendance figures. ICCC 2026 — Coimbra

Art exhibitions and artistic programming

ICCC’s artistic activity takes several forms: curated exhibitions, creative-submission tracks, demonstrations, performances, installations, and public-facing sound-art programs.

  1. ICCC 2014 — Welcome reception and art exhibition. The conference brochure documents an art exhibition associated with the welcome reception in Ljubljana. Conference brochure
  2. ICCC 2019 — Creative submissions and exhibition. The proceedings document creative submissions accepted for exhibition, alongside demonstrations and artistic programming. ICCC 2019 proceedings
  3. ICCC 2020 — SoundArt event and digital sound-art exhibition. The online program included a SoundArt event, while digital sound artworks were connected with the Coimbra festival Dar a Ouvir and an exhibition at Convento São Francisco. ICCC 2020 program
  4. ICCC 2021 Art Exhibition. A virtual exhibition curated by Lilla LoCurto and Bill Outcault, featuring work by 15 artists. Exhibits included interactive websites, videos, images, and projects involving AI, computational systems, and creative technology. Exhibition page · Catalogue
