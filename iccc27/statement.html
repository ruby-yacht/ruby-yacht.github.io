ICCC 2027 · Exhibition proposal

Exhibition Statement

An exhibition of computational creativity, artistic practice, and the changing relationships between people and machines.

short draft

Short exhibition statement

This exhibition brings artists into conversation with the systems, data, labor, and cultural ideas that shape creative technologies. Through experimentation, interpretation, play, and thoughtful critique, artists explore how these tools influence imagination, authorship, collaboration, and the ways we make meaning together.

Longer version

Long exhibition statement

This exhibition presents computational creativity as a space where technical research, artistic practice, and cultural reflection meet. The artists featured here work with artificial intelligence, generative media, robotics, software, data, performance, and interactive systems, approaching computation as a tool, material, collaborator, and subject of inquiry.

Their work explores how creative technologies shape imagination, authorship, labor, identity, and collaboration, while also asking how these systems reflect the values and assumptions of the people and institutions that design them.

Some artists take a culture-jamming approach, using humor, reinterpretation, playful disruption, and unexpected forms of participation to reveal how technologies shape everyday life. Others explore shared creativity, new forms of expression, and the possibilities of working alongside machines.

Together, these practices invite audiences to see computational creativity not simply as automated production, but as an evolving cultural conversation about how people and technologies make meaning together.

Call draft

Call to artists and designers: In the second paragraph, we wouldn't include "works in progress", "experiments" or "speculative designs". For us, a show should have finished works, especially for an audience like ICCC. It might not make sense to them when one says it's not finished. Also, we think we're going to get a lot of work to look at with the categories you have mentioned below.

We invite artists and designers working with computation, digital media, artificial intelligence, robotics, software, data, networks, games, performance, and interactive systems to propose work for an exhibition connected to ICCC 2027.

We are interested in practices that use new technologies as artistic materials, collaborators, subjects, or environments. Proposals may include finished works, works in progress, installations, performances, screenings, participatory projects, experiments, or speculative designs. We welcome projects that explore how computational systems shape creativity, culture, identity, labor, authorship, and everyday life.

The exhibition will make room for different approaches: curious, playful, collaborative, critical, poetic, humorous, or openly disruptive. Artists may use creative technologies in unexpected ways, question their assumptions, reveal their limits, or invite audiences to imagine other relationships between people and machines. We especially welcome work that connects technical experimentation with lived experience, social context, and cultural meaning.
